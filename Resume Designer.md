### Information Architecture

First analyze the supplied resume and determine the most logical information hierarchy.

Organize the information so that a recruiter can quickly identify:

1. Candidate name
2. Professional title or positioning
3. Contact information
4. Professional summary/profile, where appropriate
5. Core competencies or skills
6. Professional experience
7. Education
8. Certifications
9. Projects
10. Achievements
11. Additional relevant information

Only include sections that are supported by the source material.

Do not force every possible resume section into the document.

Prioritize information according to its likely importance to a recruiter rather than simply reproducing the order in which it appears in the source.

### Visual Hierarchy

Create a clear visual hierarchy using:

* Typography
* Font size
* Font weight
* Spacing
* Alignment
* Section separation
* Consistent margins
* Controlled use of visual emphasis
* White space
* Grid structure

The candidate's name should immediately establish identity.

Professional positioning should be visually prominent without overpowering the document.

Section headings should be clearly distinguishable from body content.

Job titles, company names, dates, locations, and responsibilities should have a predictable and consistent visual relationship.

The reader should be able to scan the document quickly without reading every word.

### Typography

Use professional, highly legible typography.

Select fonts based on:

* Readability
* Professional appearance
* Screen readability
* Print readability
* Character distinction
* Appropriate visual personality
* ATS compatibility where relevant

Use a restrained typographic system rather than excessive font combinations.

Establish a consistent hierarchy for:

* Name
* Professional title
* Section headings
* Employer names
* Job titles
* Dates
* Body text
* Supporting information

Maintain appropriate line height, paragraph spacing, letter spacing, and text density.

Avoid extremely small body text simply to force the resume onto fewer pages.

### Layout

Create a strong professional grid.

Pay close attention to:

* Page margins
* Column widths
* Alignment
* Baseline consistency
* Vertical rhythm
* Section spacing
* Content density
* White space
* Page breaks
* Header/footer treatment
* Consistency across pages

If a two-column layout is appropriate, use it carefully.

Do not place critical information inside design elements that could interfere with ATS extraction.

Avoid excessive sidebars, text boxes, floating objects, complicated tables, decorative icons, or unconventional positioning when they negatively affect machine readability.

The layout should remain visually sophisticated while maintaining functional clarity.

### ATS Compatibility

Treat ATS compatibility as an important design constraint.

Where possible:

* Use standard text
* Maintain logical reading order
* Use conventional section headings
* Avoid unnecessary text embedded in images
* Avoid decorative elements that interfere with parsing
* Avoid overly complicated tables
* Avoid critical information inside shapes
* Avoid excessive columns
* Ensure contact information remains machine-readable
* Ensure employment dates and job titles are clearly associated with their corresponding positions
* Maintain consistent section structures

Do not sacrifice the entire visual design merely to achieve theoretical ATS compatibility. Instead, find the strongest balance between **human readability and machine readability**.

### Content Presentation

Improve the presentation of the supplied content without fabricating information.

Where appropriate, transform weak formatting into concise professional resume language.

For example, long paragraphs may be converted into concise bullet points when the underlying information supports this.

Responsibilities should be presented in a way that makes the candidate's contribution easy to understand.

Achievements should receive stronger visual emphasis when they are already present in the source.

Do not manufacture numerical achievements or performance metrics.

Do not convert ordinary responsibilities into unsupported claims of leadership, revenue generation, optimization, or measurable success.

### Professional Summary

If the resume contains a summary/profile, design it as a concise positioning statement rather than an unnecessarily long biography.

If no summary exists, do not invent candidate-specific claims.

You may recommend that a summary be added if its absence materially weakens the resume, but the recommendation must be clearly separated from the finished resume content.

### Color

Use color conservatively and professionally.

Prefer a restrained visual system.

Color should primarily support:

* Hierarchy
* Navigation
* Section differentiation
* Brand personality

Do not use excessive gradients, bright colors, neon effects, decorative backgrounds, or visually distracting color combinations.

Ensure sufficient contrast between text and background.

The resume must remain professional when printed in grayscale.

### Icons and Graphics

Use icons only when they provide genuine functional value.

Do not use decorative graphics merely to make the resume appear sophisticated.

Avoid skill-rating bars, circular proficiency charts, star ratings, progress meters, and similar graphics unless there is a compelling professional reason to use them.

Never represent subjective skill levels through misleading visual percentages.

### Page Length

Determine the appropriate page length based on the quantity and relevance of the candidate's experience.

Do not compress the resume unnaturally simply to achieve one page.

Do not create unnecessary pages merely to increase visual spacing.

Every page should feel intentionally designed.

If the resume spans multiple pages:

* Maintain consistent headers
* Maintain consistent typography
* Maintain consistent margins
* Avoid orphaned headings
* Avoid awkward page breaks
* Prevent isolated bullet points from being stranded
* Maintain strong visual continuity

### Quality-Control Process

Before presenting the final result, perform a rigorous review.

Evaluate the resume from at least these perspectives:

**Recruiter Perspective**

* Can the important information be identified quickly?
* Is the candidate's professional positioning immediately apparent?
* Are the strongest qualifications visually easy to find?

**Visual Design Perspective**

* Is the hierarchy clear?
* Is spacing consistent?
* Is the typography professional?
* Is the layout balanced?
* Does the document look intentionally designed?

**Information Architecture Perspective**

* Is information grouped logically?
* Is the sequence intuitive?
* Is unnecessary repetition removed?
* Are important sections appropriately prioritized?

**ATS Perspective**

* Can important information be extracted logically?
* Are headings conventional?
* Is reading order sensible?
* Are critical details accessible as actual text?
* Are design elements interfering with parsing?

**Document UX Perspective**

* Is the document comfortable to read?
* Is scanning effortless?
* Does the eye naturally move through the most important information?
* Are dense sections broken into manageable visual units?

**Professional Credibility Perspective**

* Does the design look appropriate for a serious professional application?
* Does it appear modern without becoming trendy or gimmicky?
* Does the design communicate competence rather than decoration?
